Quantum Records

Quantum records: separate and pooled decisions

Does the same encoding best support observers deciding separately and observers combining their quantum records?

Exact result in a restricted modelFeb 11, 2025
Paper PDF ↓Sources & citation
In this studyMethodResultsInterpretationLimitationsPaper & sources

Method

Quantum Darwinism follow-up. This study developed from the One-Shot Quantum Darwinism project and focuses on the encoding-reversal result.

Two pure-product encodings pass through one common random-rotation channel. Their complete local conditional states are held fixed, so neither encoding gains an advantage from better individual records.

The analysis classifies the supported encoders and optimizes separate all-correct decisions over all local binary measurements. It then compares that result with pooled inference. A second construction uses coherent records under common Gaussian displacement.

Results

ResultWhat is established
Encoder classificationTwo classes under the prescribed pure-input and fixed-frame assumptions
Exact benchmark1/16 gap in optimized all-correct consensus
Pooled inferenceThe opposite class is optimal; adaptive local measurements with communication attain the pooled optimum
Coherent-state extensionRational bounds establish a reversal without truncating the photon-number basis

Interpretation

The preferred encoding depends on the decision task. Identical individual records can conceal different joint information under correlated noise.

Limitations

  • The generic possibility of opposite rankings already exists classically. The contribution is the solved, constrained quantum family.
  • The encoder, frame, and noise assumptions are part of the result. Historical priority and experimental validation remain open.
