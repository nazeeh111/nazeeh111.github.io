Spectral Ambiguity

Choosing measurements for positive spectra

Can reusing adversarial spectra make minimax measurement selection cheaper, and does that improve acquisition?

Acquisition target missedApr 29, 2025
Paper PDF ↓Sources & citation
In this studyMethodResultsInterpretationLimitationsPaper & sources

Method

A shared witness cache bounds candidate noisy-correlator measurements before invoking a full optimization oracle. Compare the finite-menu choice with a persistent, warm-started HiGHS baseline and a simpler ray cache.

A separate physical acquisition study compares uncertainty reduction. A corrective follow-up makes selection and reported intervals use the same endpoint-cell model, then checks the unchanged target on fresh cases.

Results

StudyResult
Original finite-menu selection5.16× median speedup across 18 held-out menus
Original acquisition target4/9 cases reached vs 6/9 for conventional target-specific design
Corrective follow-upAll five policies miss the target on all nine new cases
Follow-up diagnosisCoarse-cell ambiguity remains above target even with all available observations

Interpretation

The original computation is faster on the measured menus. That does not establish better scientific acquisition. The corrective experiment also exposes a resolution bottleneck that makes its ranking comparison inconclusive.

Limitations

  • Optimal recovery, positivity bounds, and oracle caching have established precedents. Priority for this specialization is unresolved.
  • Independent physical per-time sampling costs are not established. The two reports retain separate models, timings, and outcomes.
